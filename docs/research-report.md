# Build the carousel engine, not the scraper

Build the tool as a provenance-tracked pipeline in which a pasted link identifies the listing, while the photos arrive through uploads, a photographer's delivery link, a licensed feed, or a capture from the user's own browser. Server-side fetching from Zillow or HAR is the cheapest part technically, at pennies per listing through rented scrapers, and the most dangerous part legally, because listing photos have owners who can sue per photo. For image quality, default to a faithful 2x upscale (Topaz Precision at about $0.05 per image) behind an automated fidelity gate, and keep generative upscalers away from anything that could misrepresent the property. Beauty comes mostly from selection, crops and layout: a 1536 px Zillow photo needs only about 1.3x enlargement to fill a 1080x1350 slide, so the upscaler matters most for small sources and two-slide pans. Variable cost is under a dollar per listing on my estimates, and the visible market gap is photo-first automated carousels. None of this has been tested on live listings, no independent real-estate upscaling benchmark exists, and the legal ratings below are extrapolations rather than legal advice, so a short bake-off and a counsel review should precede launch.

## Portals cap photos near 1536 px and defend them actively

Source resolution sets the ceiling on everything downstream. The likely quality order is **photographer original, then MLS original via a feed, then Zillow at 1536 px, then smaller portal renditions**. That order is inferred, since nobody has measured one listing across all sources. Zillow is the best documented: gallery data sits in the `__NEXT_DATA__` JSON ([Scrapfly](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/how-to-scrape-zillow), a vendor), and every source found tops out at **1,536 px wide** ([Apify themineworks](https://apify.com/themineworks/zillow-photos)). Suffix meanings conflict between sources, so collect every candidate URL and keep the largest by decoded dimensions, which is what the [community script's October 2026 version](https://gist.github.com/bryant988/9510cff838d86dcefa3b9ea3835b8552) does after repeated breakage from page changes.

Redfin exposes a `mediaBrowserInfo` gallery with four sizes ([redfin-mcp](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/chrischall/redfin-mcp/tools/redfin_get_property_photos)) and a long-lived `bigphoto` URL pattern ([gist](https://gist.github.com/troy/184547)), with pixel size unconfirmed. Realtor.com has a Python library, [HomeHarvest](https://github.com/ZacharyHampton/HomeHarvest), whose README is inconsistent and contains text aimed at persuading AI models, so test it rather than trust it. **HAR is the blind spot.** The only size evidence is one local photographer's undated belief that the [maximum is 1024 x 768](https://har.com/question/888_what-is-the-optimum-width-height-and-ppi-for-real-estate-photos), and no vendor product has confirmed photo output: [Bright Data's HAR page](https://brightdata.com/products/web-scraper/har) lists no photo fields. Spending an hour in devtools on 5 to 10 HAR listings in a normal browser would settle more than further desk research.

All major portals run bot defenses, and sources disagree on which vendors. Zillow is attributed to [PerimeterX](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/how-to-scrape-zillow) or to [Imperva](https://dev.to/agenthustler/zillow-scraping-in-2026-anti-bot-defenses-api-alternatives-and-benchmark-results-17ll), while ScrapeOps rates [Redfin 7.5/10](https://scrapeops.io/websites/redfin/) and [Realtor.com 8.5/10](https://scrapeops.io/websites/realtor/) for difficulty. Marketplace run statistics also overstate reliability: one actor showing 100% run success [refused a Beverly Hills listing in 2 of 4 author tests](https://apify.com/themineworks/zillow-photos). Only a test of about 100 listings per site yields a figure you can plan around.

Official feeds offer the best resolution and the slowest path. Spark's API goes [up to 2048x1600 plus the original as `UriLarge`](https://www.sparkplatform.com/docs/api_services/listings/photos), at [$50 per month per MLS with each MLS's permission](https://sparkplatform.com/docs/overview/faq). No source says whether HAR is on Spark, and no HAR fee schedule is public. HRIS does offer a [three-way third-party services agreement](https://www.harconnect.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/3-Way-Third-Party-Services-Agreement-2022c-1.pdf), which makes a broker-sponsored license the realistic Houston route. Timing is my estimate of weeks to months.

## Legal exposure depends on who copies the photo and whose listing it is

The photographer usually owns the copyright, and the agent typically holds a license ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/legal/copyright/who-owns-your-property-photos)). Whether upscaling creates a derivative work is undecided and mostly beside the point, because copying and republishing a full image already implicates reproduction and display rights. Three precedents frame the risk. In [VHT v. Zillow](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2019/04/vht-inc-v-zillow-group-inc), full-size searchable display was not fair use, while agent-uploaded, rights-certified photos on an automated platform did not make Zillow a direct infringer. A tool whose own servers fetch and transform photos gives up that protective fact (my inference). The remand produced [$1,927,200 at $200 to $800 per photo](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2022/02/vht-v-zillow), and the Ninth Circuit later confirmed [each photo can support its own award](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/22-35147/22-35147-2023-06-07.html). In Texas, [Stross v. Redfin](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/unpub/17/17-50046.0.pdf) limited a defendant to the sublicense it actually received, and the same photographer [sued Compass and Side brokers in Austin in 2025](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/listing-photos-at-center-of-lawsuit-naming-compass-side/) (allegations, with no outcome reported).

Pro-scraper wins such as [Meta v. Bright Data](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-1470808/) do not rescue this product, because they concern platform contract claims over public data, not a photo owner's copyright claim. Evasion has also become its own liability track: [Reddit v. SerpApi](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc) (S.D.N.Y., July 2026) held at the pleading stage that CAPTCHA and bot-detection systems can be Section 1201 access controls, although a parallel [Google v. SerpApi](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc) DMCA claim was dismissed. [Cox v. Sony](https://www.orrick.com/en/news/2026/03/cox-communications-secures-historic-supreme-court-victory-in-internet-copyright-case) helps a general-purpose editor by requiring intent for contributory liability, but marketing built around "paste any Zillow link" looks like the inducement it describes (my inference), and vicarious liability was not before the Court.

Terms and MLS rules tighten further. [Redfin's terms](https://www.redfin.com/about/terms-of-use) bar automated crawling and derivative works, and [HAR-side terms read through a mirror](https://www.harconnect.com/houston-realtor/terms-of-use/) name screen scraping and limit photos to personal, non-commercial use. Zillow's restrictions were read only through a [third-party capture](https://conductatlas.com/platform/zillow/zillow-terms-of-use/provision/CA-P-032398/prohibition-on-automated-scraping-and-queries/), and Realtor.com's were not obtained. The [June 2026 HRIS MLS Rules](https://content.harstatic.com/mls/pdf/MLSRules.pdf) are stricter than most summaries suggest. IDX covers websites, apps and audio devices, not social posts (Rule 18). Advertising another firm's listing needs its consent (2.7), altering it needs written permission (12.1), and repackaging MLS-derived information is prohibited (13.1). Rule 13.1's force against a non-member is doubtful, but it binds your customers.

The research notes disagree on browser capture, and the disagreement deserves a clear resolution. The extraction research ranks an extension as the best URL-based fallback, while the legal research calls logged-in agent sessions the worst collection path, because the user is bound by terms and MLS rules there. My resolution is to treat the extension as a convenience for a user acting on photos they already have rights to, on public pages and never inside an MLS back office. It removes proxies and evasion and puts selection in the user's hands. It does not cure copyright or terms exposure, and it needs per-site upkeep. [Similar extensions exist](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/bbikjfmkjehpcpajibajpkaijapjhfno), though none for HAR was found.

| Photo source | Max resolution | Cost | Reliability | Legal risk (my rating) | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upload or photographer link | Original | Storage only | Highest | Low to medium: turns on license scope | Default path |
| Licensed MLS feed | Up to original where offered | About $50 to $100+ per month per feed elsewhere; HAR unknown | High once approved | Manageable under agreement | Broker customers |
| Extension in user's session | Portal maximum | Low | High, breaks on page changes | Medium for own listing; high otherwise | "Paste a link" convenience with attestation |
| Rented actors ([$0.73 per 1,000 photos plus $0.005 per run](https://apify.com/themineworks/zillow-photos)) | Portal maximum | About $0.002 to $0.03 per listing | Medium, listing-level misses | High; highest in paid SaaS | Clear with counsel first |
| Own scraper and proxies | Portal maximum | Proxies plus ongoing engineering | Medium, permanent fight | High, adds Section 1201 track | Avoid |

The practical design follows. The link box normalizes the URL, identifies the site and listing, and reads facts where permitted, then asks "is this your listing?" An agent attests to role and to a license covering editing and social use, with the user, time and file hash logged. Promoting another firm's listing requires recorded written consent and automatic listing-firm attribution. Evasion is never used. If the tool is only for your own listings and never republished elsewhere, practical risk falls, though copying remains copying. A public paid URL-to-carousel service is the highest-risk configuration on my rating, and most exposure falls on you as operator. Never inpaint or crop out a photographer's watermark: [Doe v. GitHub](https://www.gibsondunn.com/ninth-circuit-clarifies-limits-of-dmca-liability-for-ai-generated-code/) makes an affirmative act on existing copyright information the crux of a Section 1202 claim, whereas [incidental metadata loss fared better on intent](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=28607).

## Faithful 2x upscaling beats generative 4x on listing photos

Real estate reverses the usual preference for dazzling detail. Invented tile, a repaired crack or a rewritten sign moves an edit from "sharpening" toward "changing elements." California's AB 723, as described in the [Senate analysis](https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/media/1938) (enacted text not read), excludes sharpening and similar adjustments but requires disclosure when elements of the property are added, removed or changed. Texas has no AI-specific statute that I found, but [22 TAC 535.155](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-535-155) bars misleading ads, and [HAR Rule 1.25.1](https://content.harstatic.com/mls/pdf/MLSRules.pdf) requires a visible watermark and an adjacent unaltered image for MLS images that alter real property. A constrained upscaler keeps you on the safe side of that line.

| Tier | Stack | Cost at 0.7 MP input (2x / 4x) | Evidence and caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default | [Topaz Precision on Replicate](https://replicate.com/topazlabs/image-upscale): test Low Resolution V2, High Fidelity V2, Standard V2 | $0.05 / $0.05 (flat to 24 MP) | Vendor says [no generative reconstruction](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/image-to-image-upscalers); no independent check. A 1536 px source at 4x crosses the first price unit |
| Premium | [Crystal](https://fal.ai/models/clarityai/crystal-upscaler/playground) at low `creativity` (start 0 to 2), with [Magnific Precision V2](https://docs.magnific.com/api-reference/image-upscaler-precision-v2/overview) as a challenger | $0.045 / $0.18 | Starting values unsourced; Magnific's docs also say it adds details |
| Budget | [SeedVR2, DRCT or Recraft Crisp on fal](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/image-to-image-upscalers) | $0.004 to $0.05 | SeedVR2 is diffusion, so gate every output. Fal's blog says $0.0025/MP and its [pricing page](https://fal.ai/pricing) $0.001/MP, possibly different endpoints |
| Self-host later | [Real_HAT_GAN](https://github.com/XPixelGroup/HAT) or [4xRealWebPhoto DRCT weights](https://github.com/ming053l/DRCT) on [serverless GPU](https://www.runpod.io/pricing) | Under $0.01 at an assumed 20 s job | Worth it only at volume or to control weights |
| Optional disclosed mode only | Magnific Creative, high-creativity Clarity, FLUX upscalers | Up to [$1.12 at 4x](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/flux-vision-upscaler) | Excluded from the default path |

Evidence for these picks is thin, and I hold them as starting positions. The closest test found is [one PhotoWorkout architecture image](https://www.photoworkout.com/?p=222576) (affiliate links disclosed): Gigapixel Standard V2 at 4x resolved window mullions in 6 seconds with "slight regularization of the repeating pattern," while a generative model rewrote script lettering into plausible wrong characters. [NTIRE 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14558) is the only rigorous benchmark and uses clean bicubic inputs, not compressed listing JPEGs. A HAT-based cascade leading both of its tracks shows non-diffusion methods stay competitive, but only on clean inputs. Commercial APIs do not enter it. No independent test on tile, wood grain, window views or MLS-style JPEGs was found, so **the bake-off is mandatory**. Take 30 to 50 genuinely high-resolution images, downscale to 1024 px, apply JPEG at quality 70 to 80, upscale, then score with full-reference metrics and visual review. The notes estimate under $50 in total.

Order matters at one well-supported point: [blocking artifacts get enhanced by super-resolution](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.06993), so clean JPEG damage first when the source needs it. Portal copies may also be recompressed, which makes this step likely on scraped inputs. Plain PSNR weights trained on bicubic degradation need a deblocking pass or real-degradation weights.

Licensing removes several popular options from self-hosting: [SUPIR is non-commercial](https://github.com/Fanghua-Yu/SUPIR), the [Jasper FLUX ControlNet](https://huggingface.co/jasperai/Flux.1-dev-Controlnet-Upscaler) carries FLUX's non-commercial terms, and [FLUX.1-dev weights](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-dev/blob/main/LICENSE.md) need a BFL license for production use. [Adobe's June 2026 agreement to acquire Topaz](https://news.adobe.com/news/downloads/pdfs/2026/06/06252026-adobetoacquiretopazlabs-1.pdf) adds API uncertainty, so keep every provider behind an adapter with Crystal warm as a fallback.

The fidelity gate is my proposed design with uncalibrated thresholds. Downscale the output to source size and compare luma, using a worst-tile score so that a local invention cannot hide in an image-wide average ([downscale consistency](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11764) is an established evaluation). Restore the source's low frequencies to remove color drift. Compare long line segments to catch warped geometry. OCR source and output, failing on any changed or newly legible text. Mask window views, screens, art, signs and faces so that only a faithful model touches them. On any failure, fall back to the Precision result and log the reason. Passing is necessary and not sufficient: plausible invented texture can downsample back to the same pixels, and [PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS often miss hallucinations humans catch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14367). Sample an LLM vision audit on premium output and borderline cases.

## Selection, crops and layouts create the beauty

The binding output spec is [Instagram's API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media): JPEG, 8 MB maximum, ratio 4:5 to 1.91:1, width 320 to 1440 px, up to 20 children. The app takes 3:4 ([reported from Mosseri](https://www.thecogrow.co.uk/blog/instagram-34-image-size-update)), but a [third-party guide](https://adaptlypost.com/blog/instagram-api-image-requirements) reports API failures on it. Publish at **1080x1350**, offer 1080x1440 for manual posting, and spend one API call settling the question for your app. Every slide should share identical dimensions because [Meta crops all images to the first](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/). [Buffer's 2026 data](https://buffer.com/resources/data-best-content-format-social-media/) supports the format mix, with carousels at 6.9% on Instagram and document carousels at 21.77% on LinkedIn, but video at 3.39% ahead of photos at 1.92% on TikTok. One sequence should therefore yield a carousel, a [LinkedIn PDF](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/documents-api) and a Reel, with Remotion [free at solo size](https://www.remotion.pro/license).

Cropping is the hidden quality problem, because a 3:2 photo loses **46.7% of its width at 4:5**. Geometry offers a better answer than generating pixels (my arithmetic). Two 4:5 slides form a 2160x1350 canvas, so a 3:2 photo pans across both with about a 6% height crop. Two 3:2 photos stack on one slide, and a full-frame photo can sit above a text panel. The pan is where upscaling earns its cost, requiring 2.1x from a 1024 px source and 1.4x from 1536 px. A single 4:5 slide from 1024x683 needs about 2x, and from 1536x1024 only about 1.3x. **That makes 2x the right default and 4x mostly wasted on social output.** Exclude outpainting from v1, since invented cabinetry or ceiling height plausibly falls under "add or change elements."

Automate selection with one embedding that serves several jobs. Embed all 25 to 40 photos once and assign rooms with a [real-estate CLIP model](https://huggingface.co/strollingorange/roomLuxuryAnnotater) or a commercial classifier such as [Repliers](https://repliers.com/introducing-ai-powered-photo-classification/). Reject floor plans, maps, agent graphics and technical failures, and keep floor plans away from generative models entirely. Deduplicate with exact hash, then [perceptual hash](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9858628), then embedding similarity, flagging near-duplicates for review because two angles of a room are legitimate. Compare the count with the listing's declared photo count and warn on partial galleries, because [one script got only 7 photos until the lightbox loaded](https://gist.github.com/bryant988/9510cff838d86dcefa3b9ea3835b8552) and one actor [defaults to 25](https://apify.com/themineworks/zillow-photos). Score with the [LAION aesthetic head](https://iqa-pytorch.readthedocs.io/en/stable/autoapi/pyiqa/archs/laion_aes_arch/index.html) as a stopgap, and replace it with a small ranker trained on agents' overrides, since generic scorers favor drama over information.

My default sequence is a hero cover, then exterior or main living, kitchen and living as a pan, primary suite, bath or a standout feature, outdoor, a detail stack, a facts slide, and a CTA with agent and brokerage identity. Practitioner advice converges on [5 to 10 photos plus details](https://contentdrips.com/blog/2026/07/best-carousel-tools-for-real-estate-agents/), but it is unmeasured vendor opinion. Evidence on which room should lead conflicts: a [secondhand HomeJab ranking](https://thedeadpixelssociety.com/homejab-lists-most-popular-real-estate-listing-photos/) puts bedrooms first and front exteriors a distant fifth, while [brokerages still advise exterior first](https://blog.allentate.com/4-no-fail-tips-maximize-first-impression/). Make the cover a logged experiment. One photo set can also feed [a series across the listing's lifecycle](https://theclose.com/news/news-instagram-edits-carousel-real-estate-agents/) with non-repeating images.

Enhancement should run conservatively because listing photos are usually already edited, and double processing produces the over-baked look. My proposed order is select, deJPEG if needed, upscale to what the layout needs, then correct verticals and crop in a single resample, apply tone, and encode once. Sky replacement and twilight stay off by default and are labeled when used. Render slides with **HTML/CSS in Playwright and encode with Sharp**, since grid layouts, scrims and pans outgrow [Satori's CSS subset](https://nuxtseo.com/docs/og-image/renderers) and paid template APIs such as [Bannerbear](https://www.bannerbear.com/pricing/) add per-image cost. Three to five strong editorial template families should beat hundreds of generic ones, though that is design judgment and nothing in the research validates it.

Keep exports separate. MLS-safe files carry no overlays, since [Rule 1.25.1 bars legible contact information](https://content.harstatic.com/mls/pdf/MLSRules.pdf). Social files carry the [broker name TREC requires](https://www.trec.texas.gov/article/trecs-advertising-rules-what-you-need-know) and listing-firm attribution. For publishing, start with download packs and human approval, then an aggregator after verifying its carousel support, because native Instagram posting for others' accounts [requires App Review and Business Verification](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/docs/instagram/platform/instagram-api/overview). Unaudited [TikTok Direct Post is private-only](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines), and its no-logo wording could reach agent-branded slides, so default TikTok to inbox upload with clean photos.

## Under a dollar per listing leaves room to sell quality

These estimates reconcile the upscaling and pipeline research, and none is benchmarked. The pipeline notes' $0.10 to $0.30 per listing assumes SeedVR2 at $0.001/MP and processes only selected photos. Swapping in the recommended Topaz Precision default raises the 12-slide job to about **$0.65 to $0.75**, and processing all 35 photos would add over a dollar for no carousel benefit (my arithmetic). Paid enhancement is what breaks the economics: [BoxBrownie at $2 per photo](https://www.boxbrownie.com/pricing) adds $24 for 12 photos, and Autoenhance adds an estimated $2.40 to $6 at conflicting third-party rates of $0.20 to $0.50. Offer those only as a gated pro option, after they beat your own pipeline on a test set. Fixed costs and support will exceed compute at low volume and were not researched.

| Step, 35-photo input, 12 selected | Estimated cost |
|---|---|
| Embedding, selection, deJPEG and geometry on [Modal L4](https://modal.com/pricing) | About $0.02 to $0.03 |
| Upscale 12 photos at 2x | $0.60 Topaz; about $0.04 to $0.09 SeedVR2 |
| Copy and cover via LLM ([Haiku or Sonnet rates](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); image tokens approximate) | About $0.004 to $0.07 |
| Render, [R2 storage](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/), aggregator post | About $0.03 |
| Rented extraction, if used and cleared | About $0.002 to $0.03 |

The architecture suits a solo builder: a Next.js and Postgres app, Trigger.dev or Inngest for a durable fan-out job graph ([third-party comparison](https://devtoolpicks.com/blog/when-to-use-inngest-vs-trigger-dev-vs-plain-queue-indie-hackers-2026)), Python containers on Modal or RunPod, a container for Playwright, and R2 with content-addressed keys. Make every step idempotent on content hash plus parameter version, and preview from low-resolution images so GPU spend follows approval. From day one, store a provenance manifest per image holding the original, source, attestation, each step with parameters, and an alteration class driven by edit type. The manifest supplies the link to originals that [California's rule contemplates](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/06/18/how-should-mlss-portals-address-ai-enhanced-listing-photos) and [some MLSs demand](https://support.mlsnow.com/hc/en-us/articles/47635315367067-MLS-Now-Rules-Section-3-Images-Media-Virtual-Staging), and it prepares you for [Wisconsin's January 2027 law](https://theclose.com/news-ai-listing-photo-rules/). Register a DMCA agent, but do not lean on Section 512(c), which covers storage at a user's direction and may not protect your own fetching or transformation.

The commercial opening looks real but unproven. [RealEstateContent.ai](https://www.realestatecontent.ai/) takes a pasted link at $119 per month with video and approval, but its page does not mention carousels. Video tools run about [$14 to $19 per listing](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/ai-marketing-tools-for-real-estate/), and [Contentdrips](https://contentdrips.com/blog/2026/07/best-carousel-tools-for-real-estate-agents/) makes carousels with no listing awareness. Free [Instagram Edits gained a carousel builder in October 2026](https://theclose.com/news/news-instagram-edits-carousel-real-estate-agents/), raising the baseline. Competitor output was not assessed, and a tool that did not surface in searches may already do this. A price of $15 to $29 per listing, or $49 to $99 per month, is a hypothesis to test, with photographers and brokerages as the channels that also hold originals and rights.

A sensible build order runs as follows, with durations as my judgment. In the first days, inspect HAR listings by hand and run the upscaler bake-off and the Instagram 3:4 call. Over the next two to three weeks, ship upload, cloud-link import, selection, gated upscaling, Playwright templates, the manifest and download packs. Then add the link box with attestation, followed by the extension if user demand justifies its upkeep. Approach HRIS about a third-party agreement once a broker partner exists, and have counsel review the design and E&O or media coverage before any public launch.

## Conclusion

The request as framed, a link that goes in and carousels that come out, bundles an easy engineering problem with a hard rights problem, and the rights problem determines what the product can be. Extraction is commoditized at pennies, so it offers no moat, while its risk grows with scale and revenue. That inverts the usual build priority: attestation and provenance, which look like compliance overhead, are also what unlocks access to originals and broker relationships, and originals improve quality more than any model swap can. The most useful unknown to resolve is whether target agents can readily get originals from their photographers, because the research found no data on it and it decides whether a link is the core workflow or just a convenience.

The upscaler race matters less than the marketing around it suggests. At 1080 px output, a good source needs little enlargement, so the defensible quality comes from choosing the right photos, showing whole rooms through pans and stacks rather than destructive crops, and proving nothing was invented. As disclosure laws spread from California to Wisconsin and bills appear elsewhere, a verifiable claim that a photo is enhanced but unaltered should become a selling point that generative competitors cannot easily match. The remaining uncertainty is empirical and cheap to reduce: a one-day extraction test, a bake-off under $50 and a 50-listing pipeline run would replace most of the estimates in this report.
